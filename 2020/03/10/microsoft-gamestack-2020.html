Microsoft Game Stack Live @ Microsoft

A visual design refresh for Microsoft Game Stack focused on clarity, cohesion, and partner usability—shifting from bespoke 3D characters to a scalable, IP-driven system leveraging flagship Xbox franchises like Halo and Sea of Thieves without alienating indie developers.

As a Visual Designer on the Microsoft Game Stack team, I helped evolve the brand following its 2019 debut. My responsibilities included refining the visual system, developing partner-ready design patterns, creating updated key visuals, and ensuring that the ecosystem story—across Azure, Xbox, Visual Studio, and more—felt cohesive and inclusive of studios of all sizes.

Outcome

The updated visual system became the foundation for Game Stack's marketing, event presence, and partner materials through 2020 and beyond. Key results:

Greater adoption across Microsoft teams.
The modular system empowered partner groups to produce consistent assets without relying heavily on the central design team.
Clearer story for developers.
Featuring recognizable Xbox franchises created immediate credibility and helped explain how tools like Azure, PlayFab, and Visual Studio powered real shipped games.
Improved reception from indie studios.
By balancing AAA visuals with accessible messaging, the brand successfully avoided the risk of alienation and maintained a developer-first tone.
Long-term scalability
The refreshed visual direction continued to influence follow-up campaigns and event appearances (GDC, XO, internal showcases), setting the template for future iterations of Game Stack communications.

Problem

The 2019 Game Stack launch landed strongly, but feedback revealed three core issues:

Hard-to-replicate design system.
The bespoke 3D characters and highly stylized visuals made it difficult for internal teams and external partners to create consistent assets.
Lack of clarity around real use cases.
The original design leaned heavily on abstract characters rather than showcasing real game developers and flagship Xbox titles that demonstrated the power of Microsoft’s tools.
Risk of alienating indie developers.
Transitioning to AAA visuals risked overshadowing smaller studios—the very audience that relied heavily on services like Azure PlayFab and Visual Studio.

Design Process

We executed a strategic visual shift grounded in three principles:

Replace bespoke characters with real game IP.
We phased out the custom 3D character set and introduced a library of assets featuring major Xbox franchises (Halo, Sea of Thieves, Forza, etc.). These titles were chosen not for their fame, but because they were authentic, high-visibility examples of Microsoft’s developer stack in action.
Build a modular, repeatable design language.
I contributed to creating a cleaner, more flexible system that (1) simplified layout patterns, (2) standardized type, grid, and color usage, and (3) provided easy-to-adopt templates for internal and external partner marketing teams.”
Balance AAA presence with indie inclusivity.
We developed a hierarchy that elevated AAA IP to anchor the story while pairing it with indie studios as equal examples in supporting materials. The messaging framed AAA titles as “proof points,” not “priorities,” signaling that Game Stack was built for developers of every size.

Leveraging Microsoft’s AAA Titles

By incorporating iconic Xbox franchises such as Halo, Sea of Thieves, and Forza, we grounded the brand in authentic, high-impact examples that developers already trusted. These titles served as clear proof points—demonstrating that the same services available to indie teams were also robust enough for some of the world’s largest studios.

A modular, repeatable design language

To address the challenges of consistency and scalability across the Game Stack ecosystem, we developed a modular design language that any Microsoft team or partner could easily implement. This meant simplifying the underlying visual system—standardizing grids, typography, spacing, and component patterns—so assets could be assembled quickly without compromising brand integrity.
