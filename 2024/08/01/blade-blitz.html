Blade Blitz @ Hella Studios

Blade Blitz is a competitive local-multiplayer sword fighting game inspired by the tension and simplicity of Nidhogg, reimagined as a fast-paced 3v3 spectator sport. As the sole designer and developer, I built a bold, sports-inspired visual system designed to keep four players readable, matches feeling intense, and the interface out of the way.

Blade Blitz is a solo project I designed and developed from the ground up, covering visual identity, UI/UX, branding, and Unity implementation. The work spanned concept exploration, interface design, motion and feedback systems, style guide creation, and two years of public iteration through event showcases and community feedback.

Context

Blade Blitz started as a question: can you take the tension of Nidhogg and make it work with more players? Moving from a side-scrolling 1v1 to a top-down 3v3 introduced real design problems. With more freedom of movement, players could avoid combat entirely. Arenas needed to funnel engagement. The HUD had to stay readable across 6 characters in motion. And the whole thing needed to feel like a sport, not just a brawl.

Visual System

The aesthetic is built around one idea: this is a sport. Bold, high-contrast, and immediately readable:

Sports-Inspired Aesthetic
The visual direction draws from competitive sports broadcasts: bold typography, high-contrast team colors (red vs. blue), and clear iconography to keep matches readable regardless of on-screen activity.
Character Design
3D characters modeled in Blender to be legible from a top-down perspective while retaining personality. The model system supports per-team color assignment without compromising the visual identity.
Component System
A consistent set of UI components covering buttons, panels, HUD elements, and feedback states, designed in Figma and implemented directly in Unity.

Interface Design

Every screen had to be fast to read and fast to navigate. Players are in sessions, not browsing:

Menus
The main menu and settings share the same visual energy as the game, ensuring a coherent experience from the moment a player launches it.
Character & Level Select
Selection screens were designed for speed. Players are typically mid-session and need to communicate team assignment, character, and arena quickly before the next match starts.
In-Match HUD & Match Flow
Designed the complete match experience: the opening face-off, in-play score display, point changeover screens, arena transitions, and the final leaderboard.
Powerups
A visual language for pickups and active powerup states that communicates clearly during 4-player combat without contributing to screen clutter.

Playtesting & Showcase

Public events served as the primary testing environment. Hours of real player sessions exposed friction that internal testing never surfaces. Watching someone encounter the game for the first time, without any context or instruction, was consistently the most informative design feedback available.

Featured at Events
Selected for showcase at ECCC (Emerald City Comic Con) and the Seattle Indie Expo 2025. Hundreds of players across both shows provided direct, unfiltered feedback that solo playtesting cannot replicate.
Built in Public
Design decisions, iteration, and development milestones were documented publicly throughout production. This created a feedback loop that shaped the final product and established an audience before launch.

Outcome

Blade Blitz launched on Steam on March 11, 2026, with over 1,000 wishlists at release. Two years of public development, event showcases, and community feedback shaped the product that shipped.
